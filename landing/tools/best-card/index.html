How this estimate works

The calculator uses one deliberately inspectable formula for each card. First it converts the rate to an estimated cash value. Then it limits that value to the remaining cap you entered. The card with the largest capped estimate is shown as the leader.

gross value = purchase amount × rate ÷ 100
estimated value = smaller of gross value or remaining cap

Why the cap matters

A 5% rate on ₹4,000 suggests ₹200. If only ₹100 remains in that card's monthly cap, ₹100 is the estimate. A 3% card with ₹500 of cap left estimates ₹120 and leads this narrow comparison.

Points need a value

Ten points are not necessarily ten rupees. Convert points using the redemption route you expect to use. A transfer partner, voucher and statement credit can value the same points differently.

What this does not include

This page does not identify the merchant category code, confirm an issuer offer, read your statement cycle or know prior unposted transactions. It does not include minimum transaction thresholds, excluded categories, accelerated-reward enrolment, network restrictions, wallet routing, taxes, fees, rounding rules, reward expiry, milestone effects or the opportunity cost of using another card.

Use the result as a calculation check, not proof that a transaction qualifies. Verify the merchant, payment channel, issuer terms and the cap period before you pay.

A practical comparison checklist

  1. Open the current issuer terms or offer page for each card.
  2. Confirm the transaction category and whether the payment channel is eligible.
  3. Convert points to a conservative rupee value based on your actual redemption.
  4. Subtract cap already consumed in the current period.
  5. Enter the remaining monetary cap, not the full monthly cap.
  6. Consider milestones separately: a slightly lower immediate return might matter if eligible spend closes a valuable target.
  7. After posting, check the issuer statement rather than assuming the estimate was awarded.

Worked example

Suppose a ₹4,000 online purchase appears eligible for 5% cashback on Card A, 3% on Card B and 1% on Card C. Card A has ₹100 of cashback cap left, Card B has ₹500 left and Card C has no cap. The gross values are ₹200, ₹120 and ₹40. After the entered cap, the estimates become ₹100, ₹120 and ₹40. Card B leads by ₹20 under those assumptions.

That does not make Card B universally best. If the merchant is excluded, if Card A's cap resets tomorrow, or if Card C closes a valuable milestone, the decision can change.

Sources and methodology

The calculator follows the arithmetic stated above and does not maintain an issuer-benefit database. Read the CardCompass recommendation methodology and disclaimer, then verify the current card schedule, merchant eligibility and cap with the issuer. The Reserve Bank of India credit- and debit-card FAQ is an authoritative conduct baseline; issuer terms remain authoritative for a particular reward.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator know the MCC?

No. The page does not contact a merchant or issuer. Enter only a rate you have independently verified for the relevant merchant and channel.

Should I enter reward points as the rate?

Not directly. Convert expected points to rupees, divide by the purchase amount and enter the cash-equivalent percentage.

Does the result include annual fees or interest?

No. A purchase-level reward estimate does not evaluate annual-card economics, affordability or the cost of carrying a balance.
